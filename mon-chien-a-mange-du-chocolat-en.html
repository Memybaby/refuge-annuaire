My Dog Ate Chocolate, What Do I Do?

Updated: September 2026

Chocolate contains theobromine, a substance dogs metabolize much more slowly than humans do — that's what makes it toxic to them. The darker the chocolate (dark chocolate, cocoa powder, baking chocolate), the higher the risk.

Signs to watch for

  • Vomiting and diarrhea
  • Restlessness or excessive panting
  • Increased heart rate
  • Tremors or seizures in severe cases
  • Excessive thirst

Symptoms can appear 6 to 12 hours after ingestion, and can last up to 3 days in severe cases.

What matters: the amount of chocolate, the type (darker = more dangerous), and your dog's weight. A small dog eating a large amount of dark chocolate is at much greater risk than a large dog licking a bit of milk chocolate.

What to do right now

Don't wait for symptoms to appear. Call an emergency vet or clinic immediately to assess the situation — they'll ask for your dog's weight, the type and amount of chocolate ingested. Don't induce vomiting without professional advice: in some cases, it's not recommended.

Heatstroke in Dogs: Signs and What to Do

Updated: September 2026

Heatstroke is a serious veterinary emergency that can be fatal within minutes, especially in short-muzzled dogs (bulldogs, pugs) or in humid weather.

Signs to recognize

  • Heavy, loud panting
  • Bright red or bluish gums
  • Excessive drooling
  • Vomiting or diarrhea
  • Unsteady gait, disorientation
  • Collapse or loss of consciousness

What to do immediately

  • Move the dog to shade or a cool area
  • Wet them with lukewarm water (not ice-cold — a thermal shock can worsen the situation) on the belly, paws and neck
  • Offer a bit of water to drink if conscious, without forcing it
  • Head to a veterinary clinic immediately, even if the dog seems to stabilize
Never leave a dog alone in a car, even for a few minutes, even with windows cracked — the interior temperature rises extremely fast.

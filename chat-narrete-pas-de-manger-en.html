What to Do If Your Cat Stops Eating

Updated: September 2026

Unlike some other animals, a cat that doesn't eat for more than 24 hours is a situation to take seriously — their metabolism responds differently to prolonged fasting.

Why it's concerning in cats

A cat that stops eating risks developing hepatic lipidosis (a buildup of fat in the liver), a serious condition that can set in within just a few days, especially in overweight cats.

Possible causes

  • Dental problem or mouth pain
  • Stress (moving, a new pet, a change in routine)
  • Digestive issue or underlying illness
  • A poorly tolerated food change

When to see a vet

  • 24 hours without eating: monitor closely, try to stimulate appetite
  • 48 hours without eating: contact a vet
  • Combined with lethargy, vomiting or diarrhea: see a vet quickly, regardless of duration
